# PART 0 — ⭑ THE BIG ONE: `guarded_by_id` NULL is a *value*, not a gap

I originally called the make/miss tag-rate split (65.1% vs 76.5%) a coverage
bias. It isn't. **NULL means the shot was uncontested.** Open shots go in more,
so of course makes carry fewer defender tags. Read correctly, this column is the
single most valuable thing in the database — it's a contest flag on 100% of
shots, which almost nothing at this level has.

## 0.1 Contested vs open, league-wide (3,988 shots)

| | shots | FG% | **PPS** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** (no defender tagged) | 1,104 (27.7%) | 46.1% | **1.08** |
| **Contested** | 2,884 (72.3%) | 33.0% | **0.74** |

**A contest is worth 0.34 points per shot.** For scale: that is larger than the
entire gap between the best and worst offense in the database.

## 0.2 Where the contest matters most — by distance

| band | OPEN n | OPEN PPS | CONTESTED n | CONT PPS | **Δ PPS** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **rim 0–4 ft** | 300 | **1.53** | 756 | 0.98 | **+0.56** |
| 4–16 ft | 183 | 0.73 | 1,110 | 0.54 | +0.19 |
| three | 473 | 1.00 | 876 | 0.82 | +0.18 |
| 16 ft–arc | 38 | 0.47 | 62 | 0.66 | −0.19 *(n=100, noise)* |

**Contesting at the rim is worth 3× more than contesting a three** (+0.56 vs
+0.18). An uncontested layup is a 1.53 PPS shot — the most valuable shot in the
database by a mile. A contested one is 0.98, worse than an open three.

That is a specific, defensible defensive priority: **help early enough to make
the finisher see a body, and live with the kickout.** The open three you concede
by rotating (1.00 PPS) is worth *less* than the open rim shot you prevent (1.53).

This splits team defense into the two halves that actually matter — *how often
do you let someone shoot free*, and *how much does it cost when you do* — and
they are not the same skill.

**Adair is the interesting case.** They allow the 3rd-highest open rate (31.8%)
yet the joint-lowest open PPS (0.88). Read together with 3.58 steals/game from
Ali Schwerdfeger and a 45%+ offensive rebound rate, that's a coherent identity:
**they gamble, they concede open looks, and they get away with it because the
teams they play can't shoot** (girls' 3P% in this data is 20–33%). Two live
consequences:

- Against a team that *can* shoot, that trade goes bad fast. Both Adair losses were to Locust Grove (33.3% from three, 73.7% FT) and Kansas (67.0% FT) — the two best shooting teams on the schedule.
- Kansas is the model of the opposite build: 25.5% open allowed **and** 0.88/0.49 when they do. Best contested defense in the database.

Wyandotte at 63.5% open allowed is not playing defense in any organized sense.

**Recommendation:** ship `OPEN% allowed` + `open PPS` / `contested PPS` as a
three-number team defense card. It's already 100% populated, it needs no new
tagging, and it says something no box score can.

## 2.1 ⭐ The 4-to-16-foot dead zone — unchanged, still the biggest lever

3,798 located shots:

| distance | FGA | share | FG% | **PPS** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 ft (rim) | 1,056 | 27.8% | 56.7% | **1.13** |
| **4–8 ft** | **815** | **21.5%** | **30.4%** | **0.61** |
| **8–12 ft** | 277 | 7.3% | 25.3% | **0.51** |
| **12–16 ft** | 201 | 5.3% | 24.4% | **0.49** |
| 16 ft–arc | 100 | 2.6% | 29.0% | 0.59 |
| 3 short (19.75–23) | 879 | 23.1% | 29.6% | **0.89** |
| 3 deep (23–27) | 417 | 11.0% | 29.3% | 0.88 |
| 3 heave (27+) | 53 | 1.4% | 26.4% | 0.79 |

**34.1% of every shot in this database is a 4–16 footer at 0.56 PPS.**
The 4–8 ft band alone is the *second most common shot in the league* — more
common than short threes — and the second worst.

This is the finding nothing in the corrections touched, and it's invisible in
the current 5-zone model: zone `C` swallows 2,349 shots spanning the rim, the
floater, and the top of the key in one bucket. **Replacing the zone chart with
distance bands is the highest-value coach-facing surface available right now**,
and the data is already there (95%+ x/y fill on every month including March).

Cross-referencing Part 0: 1,110 of those 1,293 mid-range shots were
**contested** (85.8%), at 0.54 PPS. The mid-range isn't just low-value here —
it's low-value *and* almost never open.

## 2.2 ⭑ REVISED — the possession-length curve is mostly putbacks and transition

You called this, and controlling for it changes the conclusion. Original claim
was "early offense is worth 0.42 more PPS." Here it is with `putback` and
`transition` removed:

| possession secs | ALL n | ALL PPS | **halfcourt only n** | **halfcourt PPS** | % of bucket that is putback/transition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | 444 | 1.14 | 140 | 1.22 | **68.5%** |
| 4–8 | 696 | 0.91 | 318 | 0.81 | 54.3% |
| 8–12 | 706 | 0.79 | 515 | 0.83 | 27.1% |
| 12–16 | 723 | 0.78 | 695 | 0.78 | 3.9% |
| 16–20 | 551 | 0.84 | 543 | 0.84 | 1.5% |
| **20+** | **868** | **0.72** | **863** | **0.72** | 0.6% |

**The curve flattens.** From 4 seconds to 20 seconds, halfcourt offense is
essentially constant at 0.78–0.84 PPS. There is no "shoot early" effect in the
halfcourt — the early-clock advantage was putbacks and transition, exactly as
you said.

**What survives, and it's still real:** the **20+ second bucket is 0.72 PPS on
863 halfcourt shots — the largest single bucket in the database (21.8% of all
shots) and the worst.** The drop from the 16–20 plateau (0.84) to 20+ (0.72) is
a genuine 0.12 PPS cliff, and it isn't transition-driven since 20+ is 99.4%
halfcourt. That's the shot-clock-expiring bailout, and it's a fifth of all
offense.

Narrower claim than rev 1, but a cleaner one: **it's not "go faster" — it's
"don't reach 20."** Get into the second action by 16 seconds.

## 2.3 Play type — reframed as arbitrage, not error

Noted that spot-up and iso *are* high school basketball in 2026 and the league
is stylistically behind the NBA. So this isn't "you're doing it wrong" — it's a
measurement of what the league is leaving on the table.

| play | FGA | share | FG% | **PPS** | 3PA% | rim% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **offscreen** | 132 | 3.3% | 45.5% | **1.23** | 72.0 | 12.9 |
| **duckin** | 137 | 3.4% | 54.0% | **1.09** | 2.2 | 48.2 |
| transition | 616 | 15.4% | 46.4% | 0.99 | 20.1 | 52.3 |
| post | 210 | 5.3% | 46.7% | 0.93 | 0.0 | 50.5 |
| cut | 168 | 4.2% | 45.8% | 0.93 | 7.7 | 42.9 |
| putback | 298 | 7.5% | 43.0% | 0.86 | 0.7 | 58.1 |
| spot | 873 | 21.9% | 27.9% | 0.83 | 93.9 | 0.0 |
| blob | 178 | 4.5% | 34.3% | 0.80 | 43.3 | 25.3 |
| pnr | 123 | 3.1% | 35.0% | 0.76 | 29.3 | 21.1 |
| dho | 122 | 3.1% | 27.0% | 0.64 | 40.2 | 14.8 |
| **iso** | **724** | **18.2%** | 28.3% | **0.59** | 9.5 | 21.7 |

Iso at 18.2% / 0.59 PPS is the league norm you describe. The number that makes
it *actionable* rather than just descriptive is the rim rate: **iso generates a
rim shot only 21.7% of the time.** It's not failing because the shot is
contested, it's failing because it terminates in the dead zone from §2.1. An
iso that ends at the rim is fine; an iso that ends at 8 feet is 0.51 PPS.

**Off-screen (1.23 PPS on 3.3% usage) and duck-in (1.09 on 3.4%) are the two
best actions in the database and are called least.** Given the league is
behind stylistically, that's a competitive edge available to whoever moves
first — not a correction, an opportunity. Sequoyah Boys already run offscreen
at 6.1% (1.41 PPS) and duck-in at 5.8% (1.43 PPS), roughly double league rate,
and they lead the database in eFG.

from 37.3 to 47.3 and *still* holds 0.74, because nobody in this league punishes
it. That's a scouting note with a shelf life — the first team that can shoot
breaks it.
